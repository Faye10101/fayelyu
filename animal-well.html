Animal Well — A Fan Site

Overview

What is Animal Well?

Animal Well is a critically acclaimed indie puzzle-platformer released in 2024. Known for its dense environmental storytelling, cryptic secrets, and atmospheric pixel art, the game invites players to explore a labyrinthine cave world filled with enigmatic creatures and hidden puzzles. It became a natural choice for a fan site — rich enough to document, subtle enough to still surprise.

Our fan site was designed for players who want to go deeper: those who've played enough to appreciate spoilers, want mechanical guidance, or simply love the game's aesthetic.

My Contributions

What I worked on

This was a group project — each member took ownership of different pages and features. My focus was on the visual design language and making the site feel true to the game's atmospheric quality.

Visual identity: I established the site's typography system and dark-cave colour palette — drawing from the game's dim, moss-lit underground world. The deep navy backgrounds and muted accent colours were chosen deliberately to echo the game's pixel art tone.

Page design and layout: I worked on structuring individual pages so the hierarchy felt intentional — leading with the most evocative image or heading, then layering detail progressively. Clear navigation between the four pages was a priority.

Custom font integration: I integrated DownTheWell, a custom typeface matched to the game's visual identity, using @font-face — making the brand feel cohesive from the first word.

The interactive quiz logic (score tracking, question progression) and the gameplay tips content were developed collaboratively across the group.

Site Structure

Four pages, one world

Each page targets a specific player need — whether first-time visitors seeking context, stuck players hunting tips, or fans wanting to test their knowledge.

Home — index.html

Introduction to the game, visual hero, and navigation hub for the rest of the site.

Gameplay Tips — gameplayTips.html

Mechanics guide: movement, hidden secrets, creature behaviour, and progression tips.

Quiz — quiz.html

Interactive knowledge test with JavaScript-powered scoring. Tracks right and wrong answers, reveals results at the end.

Tools — tools.html

Reference guide for every in-game tool: bubble wand, frisbee, flute, and more, with usage notes.

Technical Highlights

Building for atmosphere

The site's technical decisions were all in service of one goal: making visitors feel like they've stepped into the game world.

  • Custom font via @font-face — DownTheWell_Regular.otf loaded locally, used for all display headings to match the game's visual identity without relying on system fonts.
  • Atmospheric media assets — Game screenshots, GIF clips, and an MP4 video are used throughout to make pages feel like editorial features about the game, not just text.
  • Platform iconography — Custom platform icons (PC, Nintendo Switch) displayed alongside purchase CTAs, adding a professional finish.
  • JavaScript quiz engine — Question cycling, answer selection state, running score, and final result calculation handled in script.js without any framework.
  • Responsive layout — Content stacks cleanly on mobile with flexible grid columns that adapt to screen width.

Reflection

What this project taught me

"Designing for an existing creative world — rather than inventing one from scratch — taught me to listen to source material first and add only what serves it."

Working from a game with such a strong visual identity was a constraint and a guide at once. Every colour, typeface, and layout choice had to feel like it could belong inside Animal Well — this raised my standards for visual coherence in a way I hadn't experienced in earlier projects.

On the technical side, coordinating HTML, CSS, and JavaScript across four separate pages (with shared assets and a consistent nav) was genuinely more complex than a single-page build. I learned to treat the navigation bar and shared styles as a kind of component — something I'd later formalize when building with real design systems.

If I were to revisit this project, I'd convert the shared navigation into a proper web component or template partial, so changes propagate everywhere without manually editing four files.
